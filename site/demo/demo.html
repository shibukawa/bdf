Inside a BDF file

A BDF document is a manifest and a set of parts. The manifest is JSON: it names the views of the document, their pages, and the parts each page is drawn from. A part is a run of bytes stored under the hash of its content, so two pages that use the same font or the same picture share one part, and a part that did not change between two versions of a document is the same part.

The kinds of parts

PartHoldsWho reads it
manifestThe views, their pages and layers, the metadata, the tables of dataThe viewer, first of all
objectDrawing instructions of a page or a layer: paths, text, images, groupsThe renderer, page by page
fontA font subset to the glyphs the document uses, as WOFF2FontFace, once per font
imageA picture in the format it was in: JPEG, PNG, WebP, SVGcreateImageBitmap
path, paintA path or a gradient used by more than one objectThe renderer, cached
text indexThe text of a page, normalized, with the places of its charactersSearch and text selection

Two shapes of the same document

A single file starts with the manifest and keeps every part after it, the fonts and the shared objects first, then the pages in order, so that the first page can be drawn before the rest has arrived: it is what a server streams as one download, or what a browser reads with Range requests, fetching the parts of the pages in view and nothing else. The split form writes each part as a file of its own, named by its hash, so that the parts can sit on object storage or a CDN as they are, cached forever, since a part never changes once it is named.

The hash is also the name under which the browser caches a part. Two documents that share a font fetch it once.

Layers and their roles

A page is drawn from one or more layers, and each layer may say what it is: the background of a slide, its master, a running header or footer. A writer of PDF files can leave a role out, to print on paper that already has the frame; a writer of Markdown leaves the running heads out by default, since a text has no pages.

  • A slide is its master, its layout and its own content, three layers that the viewer flattens.
  • A sheet is drawn in tiles as it is scrolled; its row and column headers, its grid and its frozen panes are described in the view, and the viewer draws them itself.
  • A board of a circuit has a view for each of its layer files beside the views of its two faces, which the viewer shows as tabs.

Tabs, pages, tables

A document with several views shows them as tabs: the sheets of a workbook, the pages of a diagram, the model space and the layouts of a drawing. A view with pages has a page number; a sheet has none, and its text goes to a search index as one entry. Beside its drawing, a document can carry tables of data: the cells of a sheet, the values behind a chart, written out again as CSV, JSON or Parquet.

Text, as text

The glyphs on a page are drawn from the fonts of the document, but the text under them is kept as characters, in reading order, with the box of each one. That is what lets you select a sentence across a line break, copy it, and find it with the search box, and what a screen reader reads out: the headings, the items of a list, the cells of a table.

How search finds it

The text of a page is stored as it was written. When you search, it is normalized on the way to the comparison, and so are the words you typed; both sides take the same path, so what you type finds what is written, whichever way it was written:

  • Compatibility forms are folded (Unicode NFKC): full-width ＢＤＦ finds BDF, and ① finds 1.
  • Case is folded: canvas finds Canvas and CANVAS.
  • Katakana is folded into hiragana: ふぉんと finds フォント.
  • A word split by a line break, or by the end of a run, is still one word.

What the fonts keep

A font is embedded once, subset to the glyphs the document uses, as WOFF2. This document uses Liberation Sans, Liberation Serif, Liberation Mono and IPA Gothic; the three faces of Liberation Sans in use (regular, bold and italic) are three fonts. A PDF whose fonts are already subsets takes them along as they are, repacked as WOFF2; an Office document names its fonts, and the converter lays its text out with the fonts it finds, or with free ones that stand in for them.

A run of text is written with the width the converter measured it at. The renderer draws it with the browser's own text drawing and, where the browser's width differs, scales the run to the width recorded, so lines neither overflow nor drift. Where the placement of each glyph matters, as in a kerned heading, the converter writes the glyphs one at a time.

How a link is kept

A link is a rectangle on the page with a target: an address on the web, or a page of this document. And what comes out

A document is read back as easily as it is drawn. The same Go packages that a server links to convert a file write it out again in the forms a system around it asks for:

  • A PDF file, with the fonts of the document embedded, plain or tagged, or as PDF/A for keeping.
  • Markdown, with the pictures as files beside the text, embedded in it, or left out, and the tables inline.
  • The text of each page with the metadata of the document, as JSON, for a search index.
  • Thumbnails drawn without a browser, by a rasterizer in pure Go: PNG, JPEG or WebP.
  • A page as SVG, with its fonts beside it; or as a raster at any resolution, for printing.
  • Tables the document holds beside its drawing, as CSV, JSON or Parquet.

変換の流れ

BDF への変換には 2 つの道があります。どちらも同じ Go のパッケージを使い、同じ Part を作り、同じレンダラが描きます。違うのは、変換がどこで走るかだけです。

サーバーで変換する

文書を受け取ったサーバーが変換し、BDF を 1 つのファイルとして保存します。ブラウザは Range リクエストで、表示するページの Part だけを取り寄せます。サーバーは文書を持っているあいだに、サムネイルと検索用のテキストも作れます。一度変換した文書は何度でも開けます。

ブラウザで変換する

同じ変換器を WebAssembly にビルドし、Web Worker の中で動かします。開いたファイルはブラウザの外に出ません。このサイトのビューアがそうしています。PDF は 1 ページずつ変換され、見えているページから順に描かれます。

観点サーバーでブラウザで
向いている文書何度も開かれるもの、検索したいもの大量に作られるが、まれにしか開かれないもの
必要なものGo のサーバー 1 つ（外部プログラムは不要）wasm モジュールと、フォント
サムネイル純粋な Go のラスタライザで描く同じラスタライザを wasm で動かす
検索ページごとのテキストを検索エンジンへビューアの検索（この文書の検索ボックス）

何が変わらないか

  • 描画の結果。同じ Part を同じレンダラが描くので、どちらの道でも同じ見た目になります。
  • テキスト。選択、コピー、検索、読み上げは、形式によらず同じように動きます。
  • フォント。文書が使う字だけを WOFF2 にして、一度だけ保存します。

「ぶらうざ」とひらがなで検索しても、このページの「ブラウザ」が見つかります。検索の前に、文書の側も入力の側も同じ正規化を通るからです。

On a server, in Go

A server converts a file with one call, and keeps the document for what it needs next: the file to send to browsers, a thumbnail for a list, and the text of each page for a search engine. No office suite runs, and nothing is spawned: the converters are packages in the server's own binary.

import (
	"log"
	"os"

	"github.com/shibukawa/bdf/converter"
	_ "github.com/shibukawa/bdf/converter/all" // every format; or link only the ones you need
	"github.com/shibukawa/bdf/thumbnail"
)

func ingest(path string) error {
	res, err := converter.ConvertFile(path, "", &converter.Options{})
	if err != nil {
		return err
	}
	log.Printf("%s: %d warning(s)", path, len(res.Warnings))

	// the document as a single file: streamed whole, or read with Range requests
	out, err := os.Create(path + ".bdf")
	if err != nil {
		return err
	}
	defer out.Close()
	if err := res.Doc.WriteSingle(out); err != nil {
		return err
	}

	// a thumbnail of the first page, drawn without a browser
	th, err := thumbnail.Make(res.Doc, &thumbnail.Options{Size: 256})
	if err != nil {
		return err
	}
	png, _ := os.Create(path + ".png")
	defer png.Close()
	thumbnail.Encode(png, th.Image, thumbnail.PNG)

	// the text of every page, with the metadata, for the search index
	text, err := res.Doc.SearchText()
	if err != nil {
		return err
	}
	return index(path, text) // text.Meta, text.Views[i].Pages[j].Text
}

The same three steps are the bdf command's generate, thumbnail and text. A document already converted is read back with bdf.OpenSingleFile, so its text can be indexed again without converting it again.

In the browser, in TypeScript

The renderer is an npm package of about 23 KB gzipped. It runs in a Web Worker, which fetches the parts, draws the pages on an OffscreenCanvas and hands bitmaps to the page. The main thread places them, lays the text over them, and asks the worker to search.

import { BdfWorkerClient, buildTextLayer } from "@bdfkit/render";

const client = new BdfWorkerClient(new Worker(new URL("worker.js", import.meta.url), { type: "module" }));

// a single file on a server: the pages are fetched by Range requests as they are shown
const manifest = await client.open({ kind: "single", url: "demo.bdf", range: true });
const view = manifest.views[0];

// a page as a bitmap, at the scale of the screen
const bitmap = await client.page(view.id, 0, window.devicePixelRatio);
canvas.getContext("2d").drawImage(bitmap, 0, 0);

// its text, laid over the bitmap as real text: selection, copy, find in page, screen readers
const content = await client.content(view.id, 0);
pageElement.append(buildTextLayer(content, scale));

// search the whole view, then the boxes of the hits, to mark them on the pages
const hits = await client.search(view.id, "canvas", { limit: 500 });
const boxes = await client.locate(view.id, hits);

A page at a time

A file opened on the page goes to a second worker, the converter, built from the Go packages as WebAssembly. A PDF comes back as an outline first, every page sized, and then page by page, the pages in view first; the viewer swaps in the finished document once every page is done. An Office file comes back whole.

const opened = await converter.open("lib/bdf-pdf.wasm", file); // the outline: every page sized, none drawn yet
const doc = await BdfDocument.open(new BufferSource(opened.bdf));
for (const i of pagesInView) {
  const page = await converter.page(opened.stream, i);           // one page, with the parts it brings
  doc.addPage(view.id, i, await BdfDocument.open(new BufferSource(page.bdf)));
}
const whole = await converter.finish(opened.stream);             // the rest, and the document as one file

The site's viewer, forms, thumbnails and search text pages are built on these two workers and nothing else.

Search: from pages to an index

Search needs the text of a document, not its drawing. SearchText gives the metadata of the document and the text of each page, a sheet counting as one entry, and each page becomes one record of the index: an id, the file, the view, the page number, the title, the text. A hit knows its page, so a result opens the document there.

What goes to the engine

  • The text of a page, in reading order, with its line breaks; the engine normalizes it its own way, as the viewer's search does.
  • The title of the document, from its metadata (Dublin Core: title, creator, dates, language), so that a hit can be listed by name.
  • The place: the file, the view and the page, so that a hit can be opened where it was found.

Two engines

The sample architecture search sends the records to Meilisearch from a Go server and keeps the API key there: the browser queries the server, which proxies the query and returns the hits with a highlighted snippet. On the site, below these pages, the same records are indexed inside your browser by MiniSearch, a JavaScript library, with Japanese text as pairs of neighboring characters, the usual way to index it without a dictionary. Either way, the engine never sees the drawing, and the document is converted once.

A sheet has no page number: its text is one entry, and a hit opens the sheet. A password-protected document gives its text only while the server holds the password.

Typography

Every face of a font is a font of its own in the file: the regular, the bold and the italic of Liberation Sans are three subsets, each holding the glyphs that face uses and no others. A drop capital in Liberation Serif, as at the start of this paragraph, adds a fourth, with one glyph in it. The space between letters and between lines is not a property of the text but of its position: each run of text is placed where the converter put it, at the width it measured.

Text set in two columns stays two columns of text. The reading order of the characters is the order of the columns, so a selection that starts in the first column and ends in the second takes the end of the one and the start of the other, as it should, and a search for a word that wraps from the bottom of a column to the top of the next still finds it.

Superscripts and subscripts are small glyphs placed higher or lower: H2O, x2, a footnote mark1. Ligatures such as fi and fl in a serif face are single glyphs that the text under them keeps as two characters, so that a search for file finds ﬁle.

A paragraph in Liberation Serif, a face with the metrics of Times New Roman. It reads differently, and it is a different subset. Its italic, like this, is another, and its bold, like this, a fourth.

Monospaced text, in Liberation Mono: the cells of a code listing line up, and a tab is as wide as the converter made it.

“A character stays a character, whatever glyph draws it.”
The glyphs of a page are the shapes a reader sees; the characters are what the reader means. A document format for browsers keeps both, and keeps them apart.

Letter-spacing, like this, and word-spacing are positions too. Small text, like this, is drawn from the same font at a smaller size. Text in color, like this, and highlighted text, are a fill and a rectangle behind it.

1 A footnote is a paragraph like any other, at the foot of the page; its mark is a link in a PDF that has one, and plain text in one that has not.

The viewer on the site shows this document as a book: two facing pages, the first one alone like a cover, turned by the buttons, the keys, a tap, or a pull on a corner.   • Enter Shift+Enter the next and the previous match

Layouts

  • Two pages: a spread, the first page alone. Pages are drawn at the size they fit.
  • One page: each page as large as the stage allows.

Passwords, protected mode and signatures

A document can be encrypted part by part with AES-256-GCM, under a content key made for that document. The key is wrapped for each reader: for a password, with a key derived from it by PBKDF2 (600,000 rounds of SHA-256); for a key pair, with ECDH. The manifest of the document is sealed too; only an outer manifest stays in the clear, naming the cipher, the key slots and the sealed parts, so that the file can be split and joined without the key. The browser unwraps the content key with WebCrypto and decrypts each part as it fetches it; a wrong password fails to unwrap the key, and the document is never read with it.

Protected mode

A server that must not hand the whole document to a reader can hand out segments: a few pages at a time, each sealed for one reader with a key agreed for that request alone, so that a segment saved from the browser opens for no one else and cannot be put back together into the document. Search runs on the server, which holds the document, and returns only the places of the hits. The viewer of the site has a mode for it, with an example server beside it.

Signatures and tamper evidence

A document can carry a digest of its manifest and of every part, signed with a key, so that a reader can tell whether anything changed since it was signed. A filled form can be signed field by field: each signature field on its page holds a picture of the signature and the signature of the values beside it, and a party that signs its half of a contract leaves the other half open for the other party. Written as a PDF file, the signatures become PDF signatures (PAdES), which other readers verify too.

  • Manifest digest: the hash of the manifest in its canonical form (JCS), over the hashes of the parts.
  • Value signatures: the values of a form, signed with the key of the person who filled them in.
  • Seals: a picture of a seal or a signature, placed in its field, drawn as part of the page.

A password-protected PDF or Office file is converted with its password, and the document that comes out is plain: a server that keeps it decides for itself whether to encrypt it again, and with what.
