The pages you are reading were a PDF file. A converter written in Go turned them into bdf, and a small renderer in your browser draws them on a canvas.

What happened to this file

What is kept

  • Text. Select it, copy it, search for it.
  • Fonts. The glyphs in use, as WOFF2.
  • Vector graphics. Zoom in: the lines stay sharp.
  • Links. Like the repository, or page 2.
  • Structure. Headings, lists and tables, for screen readers.
  • Metadata. Title, author, dates.

Try the search box above the pages: look for canvas, or for フォント.

What the browser already does

A PDF viewer written in JavaScript brings its own font rasterizer, image decoders and decompression. bdf is stored in the forms browsers take as they are, so its renderer has little left to do.

WhatStored asDrawn with
Drawing instructionsOne to one with Canvas 2D callsOffscreenCanvas in a worker
FontsWOFF2, only the glyphs in useFontFace
ImagesJPEG, PNG, WebP, SVG …createImageBitmap
CompressionDeflate, part by partDecompressionStream
EncryptionAES-256-GCM, part by partWebCrypto

Shapes, gradients and a chart

Paths, clips, gradients and transparency are converted as they are. The chart below is vector art: its labels are text.

Other kinds of documents are converted into the same format: Word, Excel and PowerPoint files, diagrams, CAD drawings, books and scores.

A sheet of a workbook is not cut into pages: it scrolls, as in a spreadsheet.
